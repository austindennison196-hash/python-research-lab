Definition · zero-split candidate

The seed step is 0 → 10. Every subsequent step selects an existing 0 and replaces it by 100. The seed convention is explicit because using 0 → 100 at the initial step would give a different first example.

Theorem · under this definition

At positive generation n, the reachable words are exactly primitive Dyck words with n pairs, equivalent to rooted ordered trees with n nodes. Their count is C(n−1).

Encode a node as 1, followed by its ordered children, followed by 0. Replacing a closing 0 by 100 appends a last-child leaf. Conversely, a deepest parent of leaves has a removable last-child leaf, so induction reaches every ordered tree. Removing the exterior pair gives a bijection to ordinary Dyck words with n−1 pairs.

Ambiguity · several rules are possible

Literal replacement 0 → 10 gives 0 → 10 → 110, not 1100. Full Dyck insertion includes 1010 at generation 2. The examples alone do not determine an exhaustive state space or a probability law.

Hypothesis · physical emergence

“Reality is a phenomenon that arises when stabilization constrains instability, allowing emergent rules to shape the formation of the universe.”

The combinatorial result does not validate that physical interpretation. A physical theory would require observables, units, dynamics, measurable predictions, and independent evidence.
